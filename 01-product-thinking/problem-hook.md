# Problem Hook & Value Proposition (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):** StreamLine Spotlight (B2C)
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** The library keeps growing while engagement falls. If nothing changes, the “warehouse of mid-tier content” label sticks. Viewers who care about quality leave, and StreamLine is left in a spending race on volume that it can’t win.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** Viewers have no sign of what’s actually good, so they leave the app to check Rotten Tomatoes or IMDb, Reddit threads or a group chat. Or they open a competitor they trust.
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** StreamLine is becoming known as a warehouse of mid-tier content, and every night our most valuable viewers prove it by leaving the app to find out from outside sources which of our 15,000 titles are actually worth watching.
- **Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** For viewers who want quality over volume, a small curated Spotlight where each pick says why it’s there. The urgency is that every month spent as a “warehouse” makes these viewers harder to win back.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** Not yet . “Becoming known as a warehouse” sounds like slow brand drift, not a crisis. need to understand -  how fast is engagement falling, and what does that cost us? Without a number or a deadline, it is a marketing problem. The real stakes: the library keeps growing while engagement falls, so content spend is producing negative returns. Every new title adds cost and makes the catalog harder to navigate.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** Systemic, but overstated. “Every night” is a claim I can’t back up yet. Also, checking Rotten Tomatoes is normal behaviour for a lot of viewers, not proof that the product failed. The stronger version is sessions that end with nothing played, or the viewer switching to a competitor. That’s a measurable failure: they opened the app wanting to watch something, and we lost them. It’s systemic because it happens on every browse in a 15,000-title catalog with no quality signal.
- **Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:** At risk of being generic. “A curated Spotlight” sounds like what every streamer already has (Top 10, “Staff Picks”). A skeptic will say we have an editorial row already. What makes it different has to be trust: few enough picks to be believable, a specific reason for each one, and picks good enough that viewers stop checking outside sources. If it’s just another row, it doesn’t remove the obstacle. The proof is whether viewers start what we recommend without leaving the app to check it first.
